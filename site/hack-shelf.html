How it works

  1. Tap + New (or press n). Only the title is required; fill just the boxes you need.
  2. Save (or press Ctrl+Enter). Once you sign in with Google, the shelf lives in hack-shelf.json in your own Drive, so every device sees the same entries.
  3. Find it again with search, the topic and tag sidebar, or the Smart order, which keeps pinned and most-used entries on top. The Copy on a closed card copies its main code in one tap.

Not sure where to start? Scroll down to Templates and tap Use this template.

Revisit: a short review, not a to-do list

Why it exists. Things you write down and never look at again fade: you forget you have them, or they go stale when a tool or a version moves on. Revisit brings those back, a few at a time, so you can keep, fix or drop them.

What brings an entry back. Every entry remembers the last day you did anything with it: created, edited, copied, opened, or answered here. Once that day is 30 days old, the entry is due and the Revisit tab shows a count. Entries you use normally never come back, because using them restarts the clock. Pinned entries never come back — you see them every day already.

AnswerWhenWhat happens
Still usefulIt is right and worth keepingIt goes away for twice as long: 30 → 60 → 120 → 240 → 365 days, then once a year
EditIt is outdated or unclearFix it; saving restarts its clock
Not useful any moreYou will never need it againIt is deleted, after a “Sure?”

Example. You save “Kafka consumer keeps rebalancing” on 1 March and do not touch it. On 31 March it shows up in Revisit. You read it and tap Still useful: it returns around 30 May, 60 days later. Tap it again then and the next wait is 120 days. If you copy from it in between, the clock restarts from that day.

How to treat it. A two-minute look once a week is plenty; an empty tab is not a target. Up to 5 show at a time, oldest first; answer them and Show the next loads more. ⚙ Settings changes the batch (3, 5 or 10) and the first wait (14, 30, 60 or 90 days).

Sensitive values

A field ticked Sensitive shows as dots until you tap Reveal, can be copied without revealing it, is left out of search, and is hidden in Copy as Markdown. It is still stored unencrypted in your Drive file, so keep it to throwaway local passwords. Real passwords and API keys belong in Strongroom; the editor warns you when something looks like a real key.

Habits that pay off

  • Name titles the way you will search: “Find what is using a port” beats “netstat trick”.
  • One idea per entry, so Copy and search stay precise.
  • Put the thing you copy most first — the closed card’s Copy takes the first code block.
  • Add a Source field with a link when you learned it from a doc or an article.
